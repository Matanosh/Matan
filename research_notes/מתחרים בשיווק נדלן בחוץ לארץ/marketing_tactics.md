## Regulatory flags — tactics that conflict with Israel Securities Authority (ISA) guidance

### Takeaway
The ISA treats marketing of some overseas real-estate units (e.g., hotel/managed units, even with land registration) as a securities offering that may need a prospectus; external marketers count as the issuer's "long arm"; promised/guaranteed returns and published average-yield figures are red flags. Several competitor hooks (guaranteed returns, "bank guarantee", group-deal urgency, yield-vs-Israel claims) sit in risk territory.

### Cited Findings
- ISA position: marketing residential/hotel units abroad can be a securities offering; brokers/marketers of unregulated projects are also offerors; applies even when units are registered in the investor's name — [hon.co.il](https://www.hon.co.il/רשות-ניע-גם-רכישת-דיור-עם-רישום-בטאבו-ע/), [Walla Finance](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3489971).
- Publishing average return rates for the project's neighbourhood/city can fall under securities rules — per same reporting ([Walla Finance](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3489971)).
- ISA findings report (6 April 2025) on investment ventures offering securities without a prospectus: external marketers are the company's "long arm"; the company must monitor all marketers — [Barlaw PDF](https://barlaw.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/summary_findings_report642521.pdf), [Goldfarb](https://www.goldfarb.com/he/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%97-%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%96-%D7%9E%D7%9E%D7%A6%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%A9%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%A1%D7%9E%D7%94-%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%99%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%A2%D7%A8/).
- ISA previously said US real-estate investment companies misled the public, using websites, social networks, articles and TV ads, and must not use ISA permits as marketing — [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001289549); ISA focus on supervising advertising by real-estate ventures — [Funder](https://www.funder.co.il/article/129816).
- ISA public survey: example of "15% guaranteed annual return" — only ~20% said they'd refuse — [Protocol](https://protocol.co.il/השקעות-לא-מפוקחות-סיכון/).
- Commentary: promised returns of 8–15% are a red flag; guaranteed yields are often priced into inflated purchase prices — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/investment_advisor_diary/news/article/20033106), [JDN](https://www.jdn.co.il/economy/2639718/); "end of dream returns" — [Israel Hayom](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/business/real-estate/article/8437045).

### Inferences
- Adopt: education-first content, named experts, risk sections, "what to check" checklists, clear sponsored labels, project facts (price, payment plan, developer track record) instead of return promises.
- Avoid: guaranteed/"protected" returns, neighbourhood average-yield tables in ads, countdown/"last units" pressure, hotel-unit "managed returns" offers without legal review, and letting affiliates/influencers make claims the firm can't control (they are the firm's "long arm"). Have Israeli securities counsel review hotel/serviced-apartment projects before marketing.
