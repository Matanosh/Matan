# Israel's Housing Market Stalls Into Winter

Between October 16 and November 15, 2026, Israel's residential market slipped further into a slow, grinding correction. Prices are drifting down, sales are thinning, a record stock of unsold new homes is not shrinking, and developers are hiding discounts inside financing deals. The operative price reading during the window showed **home prices down 1.8% year on year** for July–August 2026. On November 12, the Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS) reported that **Q3 sales fell about 6% from Q2**, that **Tel Aviv new-home sales fell about 60%**, and that **85,080 new homes were unsold, roughly 26 months of supply**. The Bank of Israel (BoI) **held its rate at 3.25% on October 21**, ending a run of cuts. Its reasons were global yields, a weaker shekel and the coming election, not housing. The **late-October Knesset election ended in deadlock**, which froze buyer decisions, hit real-estate stocks and left Israel with **no 2027 budget**. No housing tax change was enacted. Every live tax item is a proposal, and the most important is a Finance Ministry idea to **tax residential rental income from the first shekel**. Rents moved the other way, **rising about 4–5% for new tenants**. The outlook is a slow nominal decline of a few percent a year, held back from a sharper fall by bank support for developers and the chance of further rate cuts. The main swing factors are whether a government forms, the BoI decision due November 23, and what any 2027–2028 budget contains.

> **Verification caveat.** The researchers could not open any primary page: CBS, the BoI, Globes, Calcalist, the Times of Israel and others were outside the session's allowed network. Every figure below comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages and has not been checked against full source text. Where sources disagree, the report gives both versions and does not choose between them. Two scheduled releases had not been found by the end of the window: the CBS release on November 15 (October CPI and the August–September home price index) and the Finance Ministry's October deal data. Check the key numbers against CBS, the BoI's October decision document and the Finance Ministry Chief Economist before relying on them.

## Prices fell 1.8% a year, and the real decline is probably larger

No new CBS home price index appeared strictly inside the window. The reading that applied throughout it came out on **October 15** with September CPI and covered July–August 2026. Prices fell **0.6% from June–July**, ending two months of slight rises. New-home prices fell **0.4%**, and the index stood **1.8% below a year earlier** ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-cpi-down-by-more-than-expected-in-september-1001559800); [JFeed](https://www.jfeed.com/business-and-economy/tmygeu)). The BoI repeated the 1.8% annual decline in its October 21 decision ([BoI press release](https://www.boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/21-10-26ben/)). Inflation was 1.6%, so the real decline is about 3.4% by simple arithmetic. Hebrew University economist David Genesove reportedly puts it at about 4.5%, possibly using a different deflator ([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/flashes/697419), unverified). This continues a downtrend that peaked in the summer, when the August snapshot reported a **1% fall, the largest in eight years** ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/housing-snapshot-august-2026-home-prices-fall-1-largest-drop-in-eight-years/)).

Three details in the sources conflict and could not be resolved without the CBS tables. First, the Times of Israel says prices have fallen in **seven of the last 12 months** ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/housing-snapshot-october-2026-prices-continue-downward-trend)), while JFeed says **eight** ([JFeed](https://www.jfeed.com/business-and-economy/tmygeu)). Second, the district breakdowns do not match. JFeed gives annual declines in every district: Center −2.7%, Tel Aviv −1.9%, South −1.5%, Haifa −1.4%, North −0.8%, Jerusalem −0.2% ([JFeed](https://www.jfeed.com/business-and-economy/tmygeu)). The Globes figures are North −1.5%, Tel Aviv −0.9%, Center −0.7%, Jerusalem −0.3%, South −0.1%, and are most likely two-month changes ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-cpi-down-by-more-than-expected-in-september-1001559800)). Third, a separate extract gives Tel Aviv District **−1.7%** year on year ([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/flashes/697419)), which conflicts with JFeed's −1.9%. The overall direction is clear: every district is flat to lower, and the expensive Center and Tel Aviv lead the decline. The district ranking is not settled.

The CBS index probably understates the decline. Ynet reports that developers are **cutting effective prices "without saying they did"**, using unpublished discounts, financing benefits and consumer-club deals that the transaction-based index does not record ([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/hjfiwsoymg)). The Housing Ministry's director-general said on November 14 that he expects prices to fall further ([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/flashes/697419), unverified snippet).

## Q3 sales fell 6% and Tel Aviv new-home demand collapsed

The CBS Q3 release on **November 12** was the most important data point of the window. About **21,750–21,800 homes were sold** in July–September, down **5.7–6% from Q2** and about **7.3% year on year** ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-new-home-sales-plunge-in-tel-aviv-1001563213); [i24NEWS](https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel/economy/artc-israel-s-housing-sales-fall-as-unsold-new-homes-reach-85-000)). New-home sales fell **12% from the previous quarter** but stayed about **4% above Q3 2025**. Developer incentives and subsidized programs held volume up while the resale market weakened. The national decline came mostly from **Tel Aviv, where new-home sales fell about 60% from Q2**. Only 958 homes of all kinds changed hands in the city ([AgentPlus](https://agentplus-israel.com/en/news/new-home-sales-in-tel-aviv-drop-60-in-q3-haifa-tops-transaction-rankin-261112-c677)), and Globes counts just **537 new units** ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-new-home-sales-plunge-in-tel-aviv-1001563213)). For the first time in the CBS series, **Haifa topped the city transaction rankings**, with new-apartment sales up 17% ([Haipo](https://haipo.co.il/en/item/659389)).

These figures sit awkwardly beside an earlier CBS release. On October 14, CBS reportedly counted about **24,200 sales in June–August, up 8.1% year on year** ([Buy It In Israel](https://www.buyitinisrael.com/news/developers-are-winning-sellers-are-waiting)). The two periods differ, so the figures are not strictly contradictory. Taken together, they suggest that a strong June–July was followed by a sharp September slowdown as the election approached. Investor demand fits the same pattern. After a post-ceasefire rebound in May–June, investors bought **849 homes in September, down 29% year on year** ([i24NEWS](https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel/economy/artc-israel-s-housing-sales-fall-as-unsold-new-homes-reach-85-000)), after a nearly flat August with 1,077 purchases, down 2% ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/housing-snapshot-october-2026-prices-continue-downward-trend)). JNS reports **3,907 first-home purchases in September, down 15%**, but that figure may refer to 2025 and should be treated as unverified ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/for-now-israels-housing-market-is-at-a-standstill/)). No October transaction data had appeared by the end of the window.

Subsidized programs are carrying much of the new-home market. About **35.8% of new apartments sold in June–August (about 3,620 units)** went through programs such as Mechir Matara (Target Price) ([Buy It In Israel](https://www.buyitinisrael.com/news/developers-are-winning-sellers-are-waiting)), and Kiryat Gat's Q3 sales were largely government-subsidized ([AgentPlus](https://agentplus-israel.com/en/news/new-home-sales-in-tel-aviv-drop-60-in-q3-haifa-tops-transaction-rankin-261112-c677)). Open-market demand in the center is therefore weaker than the headline new-home figure suggests.

## A record 85,000 unsold homes keeps pressure on developers

Developers ended September with **85,080 unsold new homes**, about **26 months of sales** at the current pace, with **more than 10,000 each in Jerusalem and Tel Aviv** ([i24NEWS](https://www.i24news.tv/en/news/israel/economy/artc-israel-s-housing-sales-fall-as-unsold-new-homes-reach-85-000)). Israel365 instead estimates about **2.5 years (about 30 months)** to clear the stock. It also counts about 217,000 more homes under construction ([Israel365 News](https://israel365news.com/422331/empty-homes-waiting-israels-record-housing-surplus-stands-ready-for-the-ingathering-of-the-exiles/)), but it is an advocacy-leaning outlet. The stock has stayed level at a record height rather than growing: about **85,680 at end-August** ([Times of Israel blog](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/august-developers-sold-6-more-resale-fell-7/)), and about 86,000 at end-2025.

The banks have absorbed much of the cost of carrying this stock. **Bank credit to residential developers rose about 40% in 2025, to about NIS 69 billion**. At end-2025, 44% of projects financed by the five largest banks had construction running ahead of sales. The BoI calls the risk rising but "not yet immediate" ([Ynetnews/Calcalist](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/rjinoywkfx), pre-window context). Banks have their own reasons to keep developers afloat, because a developer failure would force them to book credit losses. Separately, a government panel chaired by Avi Simhon ruled that lending banks may no longer restrict developers from cutting prices. Simhon estimated this could lower prices about 10% within months ([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/bksawbqveg)), but the ruling's date and whether it has been implemented are unverified.

## Financing incentives spread despite the BoI's curbs

Developers are cutting prices mainly through payment terms. Finance Ministry data showed **30% of open-market new-home deals with delivery a year or more away carried a financing benefit in August, up from 20% in May** ([Times of Israel blog](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/august-developers-sold-6-more-resale-fell-7/)). Globes reported in early November that the share was again **above its level before the BoI restrictions**, and that Gindi and Aviv Melisron had launched new high-end offers ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-developers-finance-schemes-find-ways-around-boi-restrictions-1001561999)). A separate Globes report puts the share at **45% in September** ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-new-home-sales-plunge-in-tel-aviv-1001563213)). **The 30% and 45% figures come from different articles and may use different definitions.** Both point the same way.

The incentives have changed form. The BoI's March 2025 directive raises bank capital requirements on projects where heavily deferred 20/80-style sales exceed set thresholds and caps developer-subsidized balloon loans at 10% of mortgage originations. Sources differ on the thresholds. One cites a trigger of 25% of units sold on such terms ([immobilier.co.il](https://www.immobilier.co.il/en/blog/20-80-the-israeli-payment-plan-that-expires-at-the-end-of-2026)); Globes cites a 150% capital weight where more than 40% of the price is deferred ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-developers-finance-schemes-find-ways-around-boi-restrictions-1001561999)). Practitioners say classic 20/80 plans are now rare and that **40/60 and 30/70 structures, developer-paid rent until delivery and other combinations** have replaced them ([immobilier.co.il](https://www.immobilier.co.il/en/blog/20-80-the-israeli-payment-plan-that-expires-at-the-end-of-2026); [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/news-money/2026_q3/Article-8946b308def5f91026.htm)). The BoI has said these deals **"postponed the mortgages, but not the risk"** ([Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/20031890)). The directive **expires at end-2026**. The researchers found no BoI or Supervisor of Banks decision in the window to extend, tighten or drop it, so that decision is the main regulatory item to watch.

Listed developers had not yet published Q3 results; they usually report in late November. The Q2 season already showed the cost of buying volume. An aggregate review of Q2 reports found **"37% more apartments, 5% more money"** ([Klikat Nadlan](https://klikatnadlan.co.il/q2gapreports2026/)). Azorim reported **15 cancellations worth NIS 48 million in H1**, mostly in Tel Aviv ([baba News](https://news.itsbaba.com/story/85485/azorim-reports-15-contract-cancellations-amid-falling-apartm-95241)). After the 60% collapse in Tel Aviv demand, Q3 reports from Tel Aviv-heavy developers are likely to show more margin pressure. The researchers found no domestic developer insolvency or major residential bond issue in the window. The TASE real-estate distress news concerned US issuers, such as Aya New York's exit from the Israeli bond market ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/11/09/aya-new-york-lands-104m-loan-exits-israeli-bond-market/)).

On the supply side, labor is the main constraint. Q2 starts were **17,866, down about 10% year on year**, while a record **217,240 homes were under construction** ([Semerenko Group](https://semerenkogroup.com/housing-starts-fall-7-percent-unfinished-flats-hit-a-record-217240/), secondary). The BoI nonetheless described the Q2 dip as "slight" and starts as still high ([BoI October 2026 PDF](https://www.boi.org.il/media/h1bl2ng2/october-2026.pdf)), possibly because it was looking at seasonally adjusted data. In early November, the Israel Builders Association lobbied against a freeze on private recruitment of foreign workers from India and Sri Lanka, imposed after the 40,000-place quota ran out ([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/sjrx1yyizl)). The Housing Ministry is seeking about **20,000 more foreign workers** ([The Yeshiva World](https://www.theyeshivaworld.com/news/israel-news/2618002/building-bottleneck-israel-seeks-20000-more-foreign-workers-as-construction-delays-mount.html)). The industry puts the shortage at **20,000–25,000 workers** ([Israel First TV Substack](https://israelfirsttvprogram.substack.com/p/monday-november-9-2026-israels-construction)), but estimates of the current foreign workforce range from about 73,000 to 115,000. Slower building raises developers' financing costs and the risk of late-delivery compensation claims.

The land tenders that showed up in search during the window were mostly beyond the Green Line: 732 units at Gva'ot ([Middle East Monitor](https://www.middleeastmonitor.com/20261109-israel-issues-tender-for-over-700-settlement-units-near-bethlehem-in-occupied-west-bank/)) and the E1 tender, whose bid opening was postponed to **November 19** ([Peace Now](https://peacenow.org.il/en/e1-tender-box-opening-date)). The researchers found no window-dated results for major tenders inside Israel, and could not confirm that a new Dira Behanacha lottery round opened.

## The rate pause and mortgage demand

The BoI's Monetary Committee **kept the rate at 3.25% on October 21**, leaving prime at **4.75%** ([BoI press release](https://www.boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/21-10-26ben/); [Reuters via US News](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-10-21/bank-of-israel-holds-interest-rates-steady-a-week-before-election)). Calcalist attributed the pause to rising US Treasury yields, the risk that a cut would weaken the shekel, and political uncertainty ([Calcalist/Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/x1c9b5qbg)). The shekel had lost **3.1% against the dollar** since the September decision, and Brent crude had risen from about $72 to about $110 since July ([BoI Staff Forecast](https://www.boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/21-10-26-aen/)). Inflation, at **1.6%**, was not the obstacle. The earlier path of cuts is only partly confirmed. Calcalist and a secondary guide describe three straight cuts ending at 3.25% on September 1, after 3.50% on July 6 ([Calcalist/Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/x1c9b5qbg); [Semerenko Group](https://semerenkogroup.com/bank-of-israel-rate-cut-3-75-percent-home-buyers-2026/)). BoI staff forecast **one more cut, to 3.0%, within about a year**. The next decision is **November 23 according to the BoI's own notice**; a BoI dates page and a third-party calendar reportedly list **November 26** ([BoI October 2026 PDF](https://www.boi.org.il/media/h1bl2ng2/october-2026.pdf); [BoI dates page](https://www.boi.org.il/en/economic-roles/monetary-policy/interest-rate-announcement-dates-2026/)).

Mortgage volume has held up better than sales. New borrowing was **about NIS 10.3 billion (seasonally adjusted) in September** ([BoI press release](https://www.boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/21-10-26ben/)), above 2025's monthly average of about NIS 8.8 billion (full-year about NIS 106 billion, [BoI Statistical Bulletin](https://www.boi.org.il/media/3kefbdxu/2026-3-statistical-bulletin-pr-2025.pdf)). The likely reason is that drawdowns on deferred-payment contracts signed a year or more ago are now coming due, rather than strong new demand. The gap between falling sales and steady mortgage volume is what the BoI's "postponed, not removed" warning describes. Borrowing costs are falling slowly: the average unlinked mortgage rate was **4.81% in Q2 2026**, down from 4.89% in Q4 2025 ([Global Property Guide](https://www.globalpropertyguide.com/middle-east/israel/price-history), secondary). Prime-linked tracks are a minority of most loans, so each rate cut does little for monthly payments. The researchers found no official rate by mortgage track for September or October and no October mortgage volume.

## Election deadlock left policy on hold

The election produced the main shock of the window. Preliminary results pointed to deadlock, and real-estate and construction stocks fell sharply. Reported declines range from **3.66% for the TA real-estate index** ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/skl084kpgx)) to **more than 6%** for real-estate and insurance indices ([JPost](https://www.jpost.com/business-and-innovation/article-913120)) and about 8% for construction ([Calcalist/Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hkzfchjamg)). Around November 1, President Herzog asked Gadi Eisenkot to form a government, with 28 days plus up to 14 days of extensions. The parties that recommended him had not agreed to govern together ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/1/israel-election-when-will-government-be-formed-who-will-lead-it)). Many buyers had postponed decisions until after the vote, and the base case is a slow market until a government is sworn in ([Buy It In Israel](https://www.buyitinisrael.com/news/election-day-what-it-means-for-israeli-real-estate)). Credit ratings held. S&P reaffirmed **A/stable** around November 8 ([JNS](https://www.jns.org/news/israel-news/s-p-keeps-israels-a-credit-rating-stable-outlook-unchanged)), and the BoI said Israel's risk premium is near its pre-October 7 level. Sources disagree on Moody's and Fitch outlooks.

As a result, **no housing tax measure was enacted**. The legal deadline to submit a 2027 budget **passed on November 1** with no government in place. The absolute deadline is March 31, 2027, and Calcalist says it is now plausible that Israel will go through all of 2027 without an approved budget ([Calcalist/Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/z3g4nx8j4)). Until then, current settings carry over. Election provisions extend the **8% investor purchase tax until at least February 10, 2027** ([Calcalist/Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rexnr6xcn)). The Tax Authority chief has said the investor rate may fall, but the report is inconsistent: one passage says it drops to 6% in 2027, another that it returns to 5%. The remarks are also undated and may predate the window ([Buy It In Israel](https://www.buyitinisrael.com/news/possible-reduction-in-purchase-tax-on-investment-properties)). **The outlook for the 2027 investor rate (5%, 6% or 8%) is unresolved.**

The proposal with the most direct housing effect comes from Finance Ministry staff drafting a 2027–2028 austerity budget. They would **repeal the tax exemption on residential rental income (about NIS 5,600 a month) and tax rent "from the first shekel"**, while keeping income-tax brackets and the current VAT rate frozen through 2027 ([Jerusalem Post](http://www.jpost.com/business-and-innovation/all-news/article-914689)). The change needs Knesset legislation and faces expected opposition. On the campaign side, the Bennett–Lapid list pledged **100,000 long-term rental apartments within a decade**, 40,000 of them about 30% below market ([Buy It In Israel](https://www.buyitinisrael.com/news/election-day-what-it-means-for-israeli-real-estate)). That pledge matters only if a government that includes them forms.

## Rents rise 4–5% while sale prices fall

Rents are moving against the sale market. The CBS rent index rose **0.4% in September** ([JFeed](https://www.jfeed.com/business-and-economy/tmygeu)), and the housing component of CPI is **up 3.8% year on year** ([BoI press release](https://www.boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/21-10-26ben/)). New-tenant rents rose **5.3% over 12 months** according to the BoI's October statement ([Jewish Link](https://jewishlink.news/israels-housing-market-during-the-iran-conflict-have-property-prices-really-fallen-enough/)). An earlier CBS-based figure to July gives **4.7% for new tenants and 2.6% for renewals** ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/housing-snapshot-october-2026-prices-continue-downward-trend)). The difference probably reflects different reference periods. Tel Aviv remains the most expensive market and the one setting new highs, while Jerusalem, Ramat Gan and Haifa are softer ([Semerenko Group](https://semerenkogroup.com/tel-aviv-rents-hit-new-highs-as-other-israeli-cities-cool/), commercial source). In Q1, the northern district had the lowest average rent, at NIS 3,232 a month ([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/real-estate/article/hjekp6kgzx)). No October rent data or Q3 district rent table was found.

## Conclusion

The window showed that Israel's correction is being absorbed through payment terms and bank balance sheets, not through visible price cuts. Developers are effectively lending to buyers through financing incentives on 30–45% of deals, banks have expanded developer credit by 40%, and mortgage drawdowns are rising even as sales fall. That arrangement keeps the CBS index declining slowly, but it moves risk into 2027 and later, when deferred payments come due and the BoI's financing directive lapses or is renewed. The early-2027 test is how many deferred buyers can actually close their mortgages at today's rates if prices keep falling. Watch the BoI's year-end decision on the directive, contract cancellations in late-November developer reports, and any change in the Simhon bank-restriction ruling, all more closely than the headline index.

The market's direction is not in doubt. The pace depends on politics. A government formed quickly, with a budget, would likely let the BoI resume cuts and release demand held back since the election. That could stabilize prices in the periphery and Haifa, though probably not in the oversupplied Tel Aviv and Center. A long stalemate or a new election would extend the freeze, keep investor taxes uncertain, and make the planned tax on rental income more likely as a way to raise revenue. That would hit small landlords just as rising rents are making their yields attractive. Several releases due right after the window will reset the picture quickly: the November 15 CBS price index, the October Finance Ministry deal data, Q3 developer filings and the November 23 rate decision. Treat this report as a snapshot taken before them.
